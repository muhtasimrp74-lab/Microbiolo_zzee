## 669
**Answer**

**Pathogenesis of tetanus**
1. **Spores of *C. tetani*** enter through a wound — puncture wounds, deep lacerations, burns, the umbilical stump (neonatal tetanus), or after criminal abortion, surgery or injection.
2. Germination needs **anaerobic conditions with a low redox potential** — necrotic tissue, foreign bodies, soil and associated pyogenic infection.
3. The organism **remains strictly localised at the wound** and does not invade; all effects are due to its toxin.
4. It produces two toxins: **tetanolysin** (oxygen-labile haemolysin, minor) and **tetanospasmin**, the neurotoxin, plasmid-coded.
5. **Tetanospasmin** is a **zinc-dependent metalloprotease** with a heavy chain (binding) and a light chain (active).
6. It binds **gangliosides at the presynaptic membrane of motor neurons**, travels by **retrograde axonal transport** to the anterior horn cells and brainstem, and crosses synapses to the **inhibitory (Renshaw) interneurons**.
7. In the inhibitory neuron it **cleaves synaptobrevin (VAMP)**, blocking the exocytosis of **glycine and GABA**.
8. Loss of inhibition leaves motor neurons **continuously firing**, causing **spastic paralysis**, with **trismus (lockjaw), risus sardonicus, opisthotonus**, board-like abdominal rigidity, reflex spasms and autonomic instability.
9. The toxin binds **irreversibly**; recovery awaits the growth of new nerve terminals, which is why illness is prolonged and antitoxin cannot reverse bound toxin.
10. Incubation period is **6–10 days**; the shorter it is, the worse the prognosis.

**Pathogenesis of botulism**
1. **Food-borne botulism** follows ingestion of **preformed toxin** in improperly preserved canned, smoked or fermented food (home-canned vegetables, fish, canned meat). The toxin is **heat-labile and destroyed by boiling for 10 minutes**, but the **spores are heat-resistant**.
2. **Infant botulism** ("floppy baby") follows ingestion of **spores** (e.g. in honey), which germinate in the gut where the flora is immature, and produce toxin *in vivo*.
3. **Wound botulism** follows contamination of a wound with spores.
4. The toxin (types A, B, E commonest in humans) is **the most potent poison known**, and resists gastric acid and proteases.
5. It is absorbed from the gut, carried in the blood, and binds the **presynaptic membrane of cholinergic nerve endings at the neuromuscular junction**.
6. The light chain **cleaves SNARE proteins (SNAP-25, syntaxin or synaptobrevin)**, blocking the release of **acetylcholine**.
7. This produces a **descending, symmetrical flaccid paralysis**: diplopia, ptosis, blurred vision, dysphagia, dysarthria, dry mouth, and then respiratory paralysis — with **no fever and a clear sensorium**.

**Contrast:** tetanospasmin blocks **inhibitory** transmitters centrally → **spastic** paralysis; botulinum toxin blocks **acetylcholine** peripherally → **flaccid** paralysis.

**Also asked — pre- and post-exposure prophylaxis of tetanus**

**Pre-exposure (active immunisation)**
- **Tetanus toxoid** (formalin-treated toxin), given as **DPT/pentavalent** at 6, 10 and 14 weeks, with boosters at 18 months and 5 years.
- **Td boosters** every 10 years.
- **Pregnant women:** two doses of **Td** 4 weeks apart, to prevent **neonatal tetanus** (and maternal tetanus).

**Post-exposure (after a tetanus-prone wound)**
1. **Wound toilet:** thorough cleaning, **surgical debridement** of necrotic tissue, removal of foreign bodies — the single most important step.
2. **Passive immunisation:** **human tetanus immunoglobulin (TIG) 250–500 IU IM** (or ATS 1500 IU after a test dose) for a contaminated wound in a non-immune or incompletely immunised person.
3. **Active immunisation:** a dose of **tetanus toxoid** at a different site with a different syringe; complete the schedule.
4. **Antibiotic:** **metronidazole** (preferred) or penicillin, to kill the vegetative organisms.
5. In the fully immunised, a booster is enough if the last dose was over 5 years earlier.

## 672
**Answer**

**Pathogenesis of botulism**
1. ***Clostridium botulinum*** is a Gram-positive, spore-forming, strictly anaerobic bacillus found in **soil and marine sediment**.
2. **Food-borne botulism** is an **intoxication, not an infection**: spores survive inadequate canning or preservation, germinate in the anaerobic, low-acid food, and **produce toxin in the food itself**.
3. The toxin is **heat-labile** (destroyed by boiling for 10 min) but the **spores are heat-resistant**, so food that is canned and then eaten cold is the risk.
4. **Ingested preformed toxin resists gastric acid and proteolytic enzymes**, is absorbed from the duodenum and jejunum, and is carried by the blood to peripheral cholinergic nerve endings.
5. **Mechanism:** the toxin is a **zinc metalloprotease** of heavy and light chains. The heavy chain binds the **presynaptic membrane at the neuromuscular junction**, and the light chain enters and **cleaves SNARE proteins (SNAP-25 for types A and E, synaptobrevin for type B)**, so **acetylcholine vesicles cannot fuse and release transmitter**.
6. **Result: descending, symmetrical flaccid paralysis** — diplopia, ptosis, blurred vision with fixed dilated pupils, dysphagia, dysarthria, dry mouth, then limb weakness and **respiratory muscle paralysis** (the cause of death).
7. Characteristically there is **no fever, no sensory loss and a clear sensorium**.
8. **Variants:** **infant botulism** (spores germinate in the immature gut — honey) and **wound botulism** (toxin produced in a contaminated wound).
9. Binding is irreversible; recovery requires **sprouting of new nerve terminals**, taking weeks to months.
10. **Treatment:** trivalent (ABE) antitoxin early, ventilatory support, gastric lavage/purgation; antibiotics help only in wound and infant botulism.

**Pathogenesis of gas gangrene (clostridial myonecrosis)**
1. **Spores of *C. perfringens* type A** (also *C. novyi*, *C. septicum*) enter a **deep wound** with soil or faecal contamination.
2. Germination needs a **low oxidation–reduction potential**, provided by **crushed, devitalised muscle, impaired blood supply, foreign bodies and co-infecting aerobes**.
3. The vegetative bacilli multiply in dead muscle and elaborate **exotoxins and enzymes**:
   - **Alpha toxin (lecithinase C, phospholipase C)** — the most important; **hydrolyses lecithin in cell membranes**, causing **lysis of muscle cells, red cells, platelets and leucocytes**, tissue necrosis, haemolysis and cardiotoxicity
   - **Theta toxin (perfringolysin)** — cytolysin, cardiotoxic, inhibits leucocyte function
   - **Collagenase (kappa)**, **hyaluronidase (mu)**, **DNase (nu)**, **proteinase** — break down connective tissue and allow rapid spread along muscle planes
4. **Saccharolytic fermentation of muscle glycogen** produces **hydrogen and carbon dioxide**, giving the **crepitus** and the gas seen on X-ray.
5. Proteolysis of dead muscle produces a **foul, putrid odour**; the muscle becomes **dark red to black**, and oedema and gas raise the tissue pressure, further compromising the blood supply — a **vicious cycle** of spreading necrosis.
6. **Toxaemia** follows absorption of toxins: fever, tachycardia out of proportion to temperature, hypotension, haemolysis with jaundice, renal failure, shock and death.
7. Characteristically there is **little pus and a scanty inflammatory cell response**, because the toxins kill leucocytes.
8. **Treatment:** urgent **radical surgical debridement or amputation**, high-dose **penicillin with clindamycin** (clindamycin suppresses toxin synthesis), hyperbaric oxygen, and supportive care.

**Answer**

**Pathogenesis of clostridial myonecrosis** — as in Q673 in brief:
1. Spores of ***C. perfringens*** contaminate a deep, devitalised wound.

5. **Imaging:** gas in tissue planes on X-ray or CT.
6. **Toxin detection** and PCR where available.

**Pathogenesis of diarrhoea caused by ETEC (enterotoxigenic *E. coli*)**

4. Both nucleotides activate protein kinases that **phosphorylate the CFTR channel**, causing **active secretion of Cl⁻ (with Na⁺ and HCO₃⁻)** from crypt cells and **blocking NaCl absorption** by villous cells.
5. **Water follows osmotically**, overwhelming colonic absorption.
6. The result is **acute profuse watery diarrhoea without blood, mucus or pus cells**, with dehydration but little fever — managed mainly by **oral rehydration**.

**Laboratory diagnosis of urinary tract infection caused by *E. coli***

**1. Specimen**
- **Clean-catch midstream urine (MSU)**, collected after perineal cleaning, in a sterile wide-mouth container, preferably the **early morning sample**
- Suprapubic aspiration (infants), or catheter sample where indicated
- Process **within 1–2 hours**, or refrigerate at 4 °C, or use boric acid preservative, to prevent multiplication

**2. Macroscopic:** turbidity.

**3. Microscopy (wet mount of uncentrifuged urine and of the deposit)**
- **Pus cells >10/µL (or >5 per HPF)** — significant pyuria
- Red cells, **bacteria**, epithelial cells (many squamous cells suggest contamination), **casts** (WBC casts indicate pyelonephritis)
- **Gram stain**: Gram-negative bacilli; one organism per oil-immersion field corresponds to ≥10⁵ CFU/mL

**4. Culture — the definitive test**
- **Semiquantitative culture by the standard loop (calibrated 0.001 or 0.002 mL) method** on **CLED agar** (or MacConkey and blood agar); CLED prevents *Proteus* swarming
- Incubate at 37 °C for 18–24 h
- ***E. coli*** gives **lactose-fermenting (yellow on CLED, pink on MacConkey) colonies**

**5. Interpretation (Kass's count)**
- **≥10⁵ CFU/mL of a single organism = significant bacteriuria**
- 10⁴–10⁵ = doubtful, repeat
- <10⁴ = contamination
- Any growth from **suprapubic aspirate**, and ≥10³ CFU/mL with symptoms in a young woman or in a catheter sample, is significant

**6. Identification:** indole positive, methyl red positive, Voges–Proskauer negative, citrate negative (**IMViC ++−−**), motile, lactose fermenting, **oxidase negative**.

**7. Antimicrobial sensitivity testing** by disc diffusion, including screening for **ESBL** production.

**8. Others:** nitrite and leucocyte esterase dipstick (screening), and localisation tests (antibody-coated bacteria) in research settings.
