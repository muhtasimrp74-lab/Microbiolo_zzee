## 557
**Answer**

**Mechanism of dry heat sterilization**
- Kills by **oxidation of cell constituents**, **protein denaturation**, and a **toxic effect of raised levels of electrolytes** as the cell dries.
- Essentially a **"burning" or charring** process. It needs a **higher temperature and a longer time**, because dry air has low penetration and poor heat-transfer capacity.

**Mechanism of moist heat sterilization**
- Kills by **coagulation and denaturation of enzymes and structural proteins**, assisted by hydrolysis.
- Steam condensing on the article releases its **latent heat of vaporisation**, transferring a large amount of energy rapidly and causing the water to be absorbed, which aids protein coagulation. Condensation also draws more steam in, so penetration is good.
- Standard: **autoclave, 121 °C at 15 psi for 15 min**.

**"Moist heat is superior to dry heat" — explanation**
1. **Lower temperature and shorter time:** 121 °C for 15 min versus 160 °C for 2 h, because coagulation of protein in the presence of water needs far less energy than dry oxidation.
2. **Latent heat of vaporisation**: each gram of condensing steam releases about 2,200 J, heating the article rapidly.
3. **Greater penetration:** condensation creates negative pressure that pulls steam into porous loads, dressings and fabrics.
4. **Less damage** to rubber, plastics and fabrics, which would be charred by dry heat.
5. More **economical** of time and fuel, and so suited to a large hospital load.

*(Dry heat remains necessary for oils, powders, greases and glassware, which steam cannot penetrate.)*

**Sterilization:** the complete destruction or removal of **all forms of microbial life, including bacterial spores**. It is an absolute term.

**Disinfection:** the destruction or removal of **pathogenic organisms capable of causing infection**, but **not necessarily spores**. It is usually applied to **inanimate objects**.

## 565
**Answer**

**Why spores are destroyed by autoclaving at a lower temperature than in a hot air oven**

| | Autoclave (moist heat) | Hot air oven (dry heat) |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | 121 °C, 15 psi, 15 min | 160 °C, 2 h |
| Mechanism | **Coagulation and denaturation of protein** in the presence of water | **Oxidation** of cell constituents and dehydration |

1. **Protein coagulates at a much lower temperature when hydrated.** Water takes part in the denaturation reaction, so hydrated proteins coagulate at 121 °C, whereas dry proteins need over 160 °C to be oxidised. The energy of activation is far lower for moist heat.
2. **Latent heat of vaporisation:** when steam condenses on the spore it releases about 2,200 J/g, transferring a large amount of heat to the article almost instantly. Dry air has a low heat capacity and transfers heat slowly.
3. **Better penetration:** condensation causes a drop in volume and pressure, drawing further steam into the load and into the spore coat. Dry air penetrates poorly.
4. **Hydration of the spore:** steam rehydrates the dehydrated spore core, and **hydrated spores are far more heat-sensitive**, so their resistance is lost.

**Practical result:** saturated steam at 121 °C for 15 min kills spores reliably, while dry heat requires 160 °C for 2 hours.

## 568
**Answer**

**Principle of the autoclave.** The autoclave sterilises by **saturated steam under pressure**.

1. **Water boils when its vapour pressure equals the surrounding pressure.** If the pressure is raised, water boils at a **higher temperature**, so steam can be obtained above 100 °C:
   - 15 psi (1 atm above atmospheric) → **121 °C**
   - 20 psi → 126 °C
   - 30 psi → 134 °C
2. **All air must be displaced** from the chamber by steam (or removed by a vacuum pump). Air lowers the temperature attained at a given pressure and blocks penetration, so **air trapping is the commonest cause of autoclave failure**.
3. Steam condenses on the cooler article, releasing its **latent heat of vaporisation** (about 2,200 J/g), which heats the load rapidly. Condensation also reduces volume, drawing in more steam and giving **excellent penetration**.
4. Killing is by **coagulation and denaturation of microbial proteins** in the presence of moisture.
5. **Holding time** is counted after the whole load has reached the set temperature: **121 °C at 15 psi for 15 minutes**.

**Controls:** spores of ***Geobacillus stearothermophilus***, Browne's tube (green), autoclave (Bowie-Dick) tape, and thermocouples.

## 576
**Answer**

**Pasteurization** is the application of **moist heat below 100 °C** to a fluid, chiefly **milk**, so as to **destroy all pathogenic (non-sporing) organisms** and reduce the total bacterial count, **without sterilising it** and without appreciably altering its taste or nutritive value.

- Developed by **Louis Pasteur**; the temperature is set to kill the most heat-resistant non-sporing pathogen, ***Coxiella burnetii***.
- **Pathogens destroyed:** *M. tuberculosis*, *M. bovis*, *Brucella abortus*, *Salmonella*, *Coxiella burnetii*, *Campylobacter*, *Streptococcus pyogenes*, *Listeria*.

**Classification (methods, with temperature and holding time)**

**Answer**

**Factors (modes) by which bacteria are killed**

**A. Physical agents**
1. **Heat** — dry heat kills by **oxidation and dehydration**; moist heat by **protein coagulation and denaturation**.
2. **Radiation** — ionising radiation (gamma, X-ray) produces **free radicals that break DNA strands**; UV light forms **thymine dimers**.
3. **Desiccation (drying)** and **osmotic pressure** (plasmolysis in high salt or sugar).
4. **Filtration** (removal rather than killing) and sonic or ultrasonic vibration (cavitation).
5. **Low temperature** (bacteriostatic, with some killing on freezing and thawing), and **sunlight**.

**B. Chemical agents**
6. **Protein denaturation and coagulation:** alcohols, phenols, acids and alkalis.
7. **Alkylation** of proteins and nucleic acids: formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde, ethylene oxide.
8. **Oxidation** of sulphydryl groups: halogens (chlorine, iodine), hydrogen peroxide, peracetic acid, permanganate.
9. **Damage to the cell membrane:** surface-active agents (quaternary ammonium compounds, chlorhexidine), polymyxin.
10. **Binding to sulphydryl groups:** heavy metals (mercury, silver).
11. **Intercalation with nucleic acid:** dyes (crystal violet, acriflavine).

**C. Biological**
12. **Antibiotics**, by inhibiting cell-wall, protein or nucleic-acid synthesis or membrane function.
13. **Host defences:** phagocytosis, complement-mediated lysis, lysozyme, defensins and antibody.
14. **Bacteriophage lysis**, and bacteriocins.
