**Answer**

**Mechanism of pseudomembrane formation in diphtheria**
1. ***Corynebacterium diphtheriae*** is inhaled and **colonises the mucosa of the fauces, tonsils and pharynx**, where it multiplies locally; **it does not invade the bloodstream**.
2. **Toxigenic (lysogenic, *tox*⁺) strains** produce **diphtheria exotoxin** at the site.
3. The toxin is absorbed by the local epithelium and **blocks protein synthesis** by ADP-ribosylating **elongation factor 2 (EF-2)**.
4. This causes **necrosis of the epithelial cells**, with an intense **acute inflammatory response**.
5. The damaged mucosa exudes **fibrin**, which, with **necrotic epithelium, leucocytes, red cells and the bacteria themselves**, coagulates into a **greyish-white pseudomembrane**.
6. The membrane is **firmly adherent** to the underlying tissue, because it forms within the necrotic epithelium rather than on top of it; **attempts to remove it leave a raw, bleeding surface**.
7. It spreads over the tonsils, uvula, soft palate and may extend to the larynx and trachea, where it can **detach and cause fatal airway obstruction** ("bull neck" with cervical lymphadenopathy).

## 654
**Answer**

**Mechanism of action of diphtheria toxin**
1. The toxin is produced only by **lysogenic strains carrying the *tox* gene** of the **β-prophage** (lysogenic conversion); its synthesis is favoured by **low iron**.
2. It is a **single polypeptide with two fragments** linked by a disulphide bond:
   - **Fragment B (binding):** attaches to the **heparin-binding EGF-like growth factor receptor** on the host cell and allows entry by receptor-mediated endocytosis.
   - **Fragment A (active):** released into the cytosol on acidification of the endosome.
3. **Fragment A catalyses the transfer of ADP-ribose from NAD⁺ onto elongation factor 2 (EF-2)** — "ADP-ribosylation".
4. **Inactivated EF-2 cannot translocate the peptidyl-tRNA on the ribosome**, so **protein synthesis stops**.
5. A **single molecule of fragment A can inactivate the entire EF-2 pool** of a cell and kill it, which is why the toxin is extremely potent (lethal dose about 0.1 µg/kg).
6. **Effects:** local necrosis and pseudomembrane formation, and after absorption, **myocarditis**, **demyelinating polyneuritis** (palatal and ocular palsy), and renal tubular necrosis.
7. Toxoid (formalin-treated toxin) retains antigenicity but loses toxicity, and is used in the **DPT vaccine**.

## 665
**Answer**

**Why anaerobic bacteria cannot grow in the presence of oxygen**
1. They **lack the protective enzymes** that detoxify the toxic products of oxygen metabolism:
   - **Superoxide dismutase (SOD)** — absent or deficient, so the **superoxide radical (O₂⁻)** accumulates
   - **Catalase and peroxidase** — absent, so **hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)** accumulates
2. Superoxide and H₂O₂ interact (Fenton/Haber–Weiss reaction) to generate the **hydroxyl radical (OH·)**, the most damaging free radical.
3. These reactive oxygen species **oxidise lipids, proteins and DNA**, killing the cell.
4. Anaerobes also depend on **enzymes with reduced sulphydryl (–SH) groups** and on **oxygen-labile proteins** such as nitrogenase and some flavoproteins, which are **irreversibly inactivated by oxygen**.
5. They require a **low oxidation–reduction (redox) potential (Eh below about −150 mV)** for growth; the presence of oxygen raises the Eh above the level they can tolerate.
6. Therefore they grow only in **reduced media** (with thioglycollate, cysteine or cooked meat particles) in an **anaerobic environment**.

## 666
**Answer**

**Why anaerobic bacteria cannot survive in the presence of oxygen**
1. **Lack of superoxide dismutase**, so the **superoxide anion (O₂⁻)** produced during oxygen metabolism is not removed.
2. **Lack of catalase and peroxidase**, so **hydrogen peroxide** accumulates.
3. These combine to form the highly reactive **hydroxyl radical**, which damages **DNA, membrane lipids and proteins**, killing the organism.
4. Their essential enzymes contain **reduced sulphydryl groups** that are **oxidised and inactivated by oxygen** (e.g. nitrogenase, pyruvate–ferredoxin oxidoreductase).
5. They need a **low redox potential (Eh below −150 mV)**, which oxygen abolishes.
6. Hence they are grown in **anaerobic jars (GasPak), anaerobic chambers, Robertson's cooked meat medium or thioglycollate broth**, with methylene blue as the anaerobiosis indicator.
