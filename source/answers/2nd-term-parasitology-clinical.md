**Answer**

**Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL)** is a skin complication caused by *L. donovani*.
- It appears **months to years after apparent cure** of kala-azar: about 2–3 years in Bangladesh and India.
- It occasionally appears without a history of kala-azar.

**Pathogenesis**
1. A few **amastigotes persist** in the skin after treatment of visceral disease.
2. **Immune reconstitution:**
   - A recovering **Th1 response (IFN-γ)** attacks parasites in the skin and causes inflammation.
   - High local **IL-10** allows the parasites to persist.
3. Parasites inside dermal macrophages, with lymphocytic and granulomatous infiltrates, produce:
   - **hypopigmented macules**,
   - erythematous patches (face),
   - **nodules** (face, trunk, limbs).
4. Skin lesions contain many parasites. **PKDL patients are reservoirs** that maintain anthroponotic transmission by sandflies.

1. **Slit-skin smear or skin biopsy** from a nodule or macule, stained with Giemsa, shows **LD bodies** in dermal macrophages. They are plentiful in nodular lesions and scanty in macular ones.
2. **Culture** of the biopsy in **NNN medium** grows promastigotes.
3. **PCR / qPCR** on skin biopsy or slit-skin smear is the most sensitive test.
4. **rK39 test** is usually positive. This supports the diagnosis but cannot by itself separate PKDL from past kala-azar.
5. **Histopathology:** dermal infiltrate of macrophages and lymphocytes, with or without granulomas.
6. **History** of kala-azar treatment.

**Answer**

**Aldehyde test (Napier's formol-gel test)**
- **Principle:** it detects the marked **rise in serum IgG** (hypergammaglobulinaemia) in kala-azar.
- **Method:** add 1 drop of 40% formalin to 1 mL of serum. A positive result is an **opaque, jelly-like (egg-white) coagulum within 20 minutes**.

**Advantages**
- Simple, **cheap and rapid**.
- No special equipment or trained staff needed.
- Useful as a field and peripheral screening test.

**Disadvantages**
- **Non-specific:** it is positive in other conditions with raised globulin, such as chronic malaria, TB, multiple myeloma, trypanosomiasis and cirrhosis.
- Becomes positive only **after about 3 months** of illness, so it is negative in early disease.

- It is an indirect test and does not show the parasite.
- It has now been replaced by **rK39** and DAT.

**Why man is a dead-end intermediate host for *E. granulosus***
- In the natural cycle, the dog (definitive host) becomes infected by **eating the hydatid cysts** in the viscera of sheep or cattle.
- When humans develop hydatid cysts, **these cysts are not eaten by dogs**, so the larvae cannot reach the definitive host and develop into adult worms.
- **The life cycle cannot be completed or transmitted further** from man. The parasite reaches a "dead end" in humans.

## 429
**Answer**

**Procedure of blood collection for culture**
1. **Timing:**
   - Collect **before starting antibiotics**, ideally at the onset of fever or chills.
   - Take **2–3 sets** from separate venepuncture sites, 30–60 min apart (or together in an emergency).
2. **Site:** a peripheral vein, e.g. the **antecubital vein**. Do not draw through an existing IV line.
3. **Asepsis:**
   - Wash hands and wear sterile gloves.
   - Clean the skin with **70% alcohol**, then **2% chlorhexidine or 10% povidone-iodine**, and let it **dry for 1–2 min**.
   - **Do not palpate** the site after disinfection.
   - Disinfect the **bottle tops** with 70% alcohol.
4. **Volume:**
   - **Adults:** 8–10 mL per bottle, 20 mL per set.
   - **Children:** 1–5 mL.
   - Keep a **blood:broth ratio of 1:5 to 1:10** to dilute antibacterial substances.
5. **Inoculation:** inoculate **aerobic and anaerobic bottles** (broth with SPS anticoagulant) immediately and mix gently.
6. **Labelling and transport:** label with the patient's details and time of collection. Send **at once at room temperature**, or incubate at 37 °C. **Never refrigerate.**

## 430
**Answer**

**Methods of blood culture**
1. **Conventional (manual) broth culture:**
   - Blood in broth with SPS, incubated at 37 °C for **7 days**.
   - Daily inspection, with **blind subcultures** on blood and MacConkey agar.
2. **Biphasic (Castañeda) medium:** solid and liquid phases in one bottle. Used especially for *Brucella* and *Salmonella*.
3. **Automated continuous-monitoring systems:** **BACTEC** and **BacT/ALERT**. These detect CO2 production by fluorescence or colour change.
4. **Lysis-centrifugation** (Isolator) system, for fungi and mycobacteria.
5. **Clot culture**, in bile broth with streptokinase (enteric fever).

**Media used for blood culture**
- **Brain–heart infusion broth**, **tryptic soy broth**, glucose broth, thioglycollate broth (anaerobes).
- **Bile broth** or taurocholate broth (*Salmonella*).
- **Castañeda biphasic medium**.
- All contain **0.025–0.05% SPS** as anticoagulant and anticomplementary agent. Resin-containing bottles neutralise antibiotics.

## 431
**Answer**

**Why blood culture differs from other cultures**
1. Blood is **normally sterile**, and bacteraemia involves **very few organisms** (often fewer than 1–10 CFU/mL). This means a **large volume** of blood and **enrichment in broth** are needed, not direct plating.
2. Blood contains **antibacterial factors**: complement, antibodies, phagocytes, lysozyme, and often **antibiotics**. So blood must be **diluted 1:5 to 1:10**, and **SPS** (anticoagulant and anticomplementary) or **resins** added.
3. Bacteraemia is often **intermittent or transient**. So **2–3 sets** are needed, timed with fever.
4. **Prolonged incubation (5–7 days)** and repeated subcultures, or automated monitoring, are required.
5. **Skin contaminants** (coagulase-negative staphylococci, diphtheroids) must be distinguished from true pathogens. This requires **strict asepsis** and **multiple sets**.
6. Both **aerobic and anaerobic** bottles are used.

## 422
**Answer**

**CSF findings that differentiate bacterial from viral meningitis**

| CSF feature | Bacterial (pyogenic) | Viral (aseptic) |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | Markedly raised | Normal or slightly raised |
| Appearance | **Turbid or purulent** | **Clear** |
| Cells | **1,000–10,000/µL, mostly neutrophils** | **10–500/µL, mostly lymphocytes** |
| Protein | **Markedly raised** (above 100 mg/dL) | Normal or mildly raised |
| Glucose | **Low** (below 40 mg/dL; CSF:blood ratio below 0.4) | **Normal** |
| Lactate | Raised (above 3.5 mmol/L) | Normal |
| Gram stain | **Organisms seen** (60–90%) | No organisms |
| Culture | **Positive** | Sterile on routine media |
| Antigen / PCR | Bacterial antigen (latex), bacterial PCR | **Viral PCR** (HSV, enterovirus) positive |

(In TB meningitis there are lymphocytes, very high protein, low glucose, and a cobweb clot.)

**Role of CSF in diagnosing meningitis**
- Confirms meningitis.
- Separates pyogenic, viral, tuberculous and fungal causes.
- Identifies the organism by Gram stain, India ink, ZN stain, culture, antigen tests and PCR.
- Provides **antimicrobial susceptibility** results to guide therapy.
- Used to monitor response to treatment.

## 424
**Answer**

**Laboratory diagnosis of UTI**
1. **Specimen:**
   - **Clean-catch midstream urine (MSU)**, ideally the early-morning sample.
   - Catheter urine (taken from the sampling port), or **suprapubic aspirate** (in infants).
   - Process **within 1–2 h**, or refrigerate at 4 °C (up to 24 h) or use a boric-acid container.
2. **Microscopy (wet mount):**
   - **Pus cells** above 10/µL (5 or more per high-power field), and bacteria.
   - RBCs, and casts (WBC casts suggest pyelonephritis).
   - **Gram stain** of uncentrifuged urine: **1 or more bacteria per oil-immersion field** corresponds to about 10^5 CFU/mL.
3. **Rapid screening tests:**
   - Dipstick **nitrite** (Griess test) for Enterobacteriaceae.
   - **Leucocyte esterase**.
4. **Semi-quantitative culture:**
   - Use a **calibrated loop** (0.001 mL) on **CLED** agar, or blood and MacConkey agar, at 37 °C overnight.
   - **Kass criteria:**
     - **10^5 CFU/mL or more** of a single organism = **significant bacteriuria**;
     - 10^4–10^5 = doubtful (repeat);
     - below 10^4 = contamination.
   - Lower counts are significant in symptomatic women, in catheter samples, and any growth in a suprapubic aspirate.
5. **Identification** by colony morphology, Gram stain and **biochemical tests** (or MALDI-TOF).
6. **Antimicrobial susceptibility testing** (Kirby–Bauer).
