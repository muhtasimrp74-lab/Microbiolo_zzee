**Koch's postulates** are the criteria laid down by Robert Koch (1884) to **establish that a particular microorganism is the cause of a particular disease**.

**The postulates**
1. The organism must be **found in all cases of the disease** and its distribution in the body must correspond to the lesions.
2. It must be **isolated in pure culture** from the lesions.
3. Inoculation of this pure culture into a **suitable susceptible animal** must **reproduce the same disease**.
4. The organism must be **re-isolated in pure culture** from the experimentally infected animal.

*(A fifth, added later: a specific antibody response to the organism should be demonstrable.)*

**Clinical importance**
- Provides the scientific basis for **proving the aetiology** of an infectious disease.
- Guides the search for the causative agent of **new and emerging infections**.
- Underlies the rational development of **vaccines and specific therapy**.
- Supports **diagnosis** (isolating the organism from the lesion) and **infection control**.
- Molecular Koch's postulates extend the principle to identifying **virulence genes**.
