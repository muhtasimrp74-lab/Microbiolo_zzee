## 789
**Answer**

**Immunoglobulin** is the term for the **group of structurally related glycoproteins, present in serum and tissue fluids, that possess antibody activity** — that is, they bind antigen specifically.

- They constitute the **gamma globulin fraction** of serum (about 20–25% of total serum protein).
- **Basic unit:** **two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains**, held by disulphide bonds, giving a Y-shaped molecule with two antigen-binding sites.
- **Five classes (isotypes)** by heavy chain: **IgG (γ), IgA (α), IgM (µ), IgD (δ), IgE (ε)**; two light chain types, **kappa and lambda**.
- **Immunoglobulin vs antibody:** all antibodies are immunoglobulins, but some immunoglobulins (e.g. myeloma proteins, Bence Jones protein) have no demonstrable antibody activity — "immunoglobulin" is the **structural** term, "antibody" the **functional** one.

**Answer**

**Structure of the antibody molecule**

**Basic unit:** a **Y-shaped glycoprotein of four polypeptide chains** — **two identical heavy (H) chains and two identical light (L) chains** — held together by **interchain disulphide bonds** and non-covalent forces.

```
          Antigen-binding site      Antigen-binding site
                  \                      /
            VL ___ \__                __/ ___ VL
    Light  |        |  |            |  |        |  Light
    chain  CL ______|  |            |  |______ CL  chain
                  VH |  |            |  | VH
                  CH1 |  |            |  | CH1
                 ─────┤  ├──Hinge────┤  ├─────   ← papain cleaves here
                      CH2 \          / CH2          (2 Fab + 1 Fc)
                       CH3  \______/   CH3
                              Fc
                      (complement & Fc receptor binding,
                       placental transfer, carbohydrate)
```

**1. Chains**
- **Heavy chain:** 440–550 amino acids, ~50 kDa; five types — **γ, α, µ, δ, ε** — which determine the **class**
- **Light chain:** 220 amino acids, ~25 kDa; two types — **kappa (κ) and lambda (λ)**; a molecule carries only one type

**2. Domains**
- **Variable regions: VH and VL** — contain three **hypervariable (complementarity-determining, CDR) regions** that form the **antigen-binding site** and determine specificity (**idiotype**)
- **Constant regions: CL and CH1, CH2, CH3** (and CH4 in IgM and IgE) — determine the class and the biological (effector) functions

**3. Hinge region** — between CH1 and CH2, rich in proline and cysteine; gives **flexibility** so the two arms can grasp epitopes at varying distances; absent in IgM and IgE.

**4. Enzymatic cleavage**
- **Papain** cleaves **above** the interchain disulphide bond → **two identical Fab fragments + one Fc fragment**
- **Pepsin** cleaves **below** it → one bivalent **F(ab')₂** fragment + degraded Fc

**Functions of the Fab portion** ("fragment antigen binding")
1. Contains **VL + CL + VH + CH1**; carries the **antigen-binding site** formed by the CDRs
2. **Binds antigen specifically** — determines the specificity of the antibody and its **idiotype**
3. Each molecule has **two Fab arms**, so it is **bivalent** — enabling **cross-linking, agglutination and precipitation**
4. The monovalent **Fab fragment binds but cannot cross-link**, so it can **block** reactions; **F(ab')₂ is bivalent and can still agglutinate** but **cannot fix complement or bind Fc receptors**

**Functions of the Fc portion** ("fragment crystallisable")
1. Contains the **CH2 and CH3 domains** of both heavy chains, with the **carbohydrate**; **does not bind antigen**
2. Determines the **class (isotype)** and all the **biological effector functions**
3. **Complement activation** — the C1q binding site lies in **CH2** (IgM and IgG only)
4. **Binding to Fc receptors** on phagocytes and NK cells → **opsonisation** and **ADCC**
5. **Transplacental transfer** of IgG (via the **FcRn receptor** in CH2–CH3)
6. **Binding to mast cells and basophils** (IgE, through FcεRI) — type I hypersensitivity
7. **Fixation to skin (homocytotropism)**, mucosal transport of IgA (poly-Ig receptor), **control of catabolic rate and half-life**, and binding of **protein A of *S. aureus***

## 804
**Answer**

**The classical pathway of complement activation**

**Activation (recognition unit)**
1. **IgM or IgG antibody binds antigen**, exposing the **C1q binding site in the CH2 domain of IgG** (or the CH3 domain of IgM).
2. **C1q** (which has six globular heads) must bind **at least two Fc regions** — so a **single pentameric IgM** suffices, but **two adjacent IgG molecules** are needed. IgM is therefore far more efficient.
3. Binding activates **C1r**, which activates **C1s**, a serine esterase. (C1 is a Ca²⁺-dependent complex of C1q, 2×C1r, 2×C1s.)

**Activation unit**
4. **C1s cleaves C4** into **C4a** (released; weak anaphylatoxin) and **C4b**, which binds covalently to the membrane.
5. **C1s then cleaves C2** (bound to C4b in the presence of Mg²⁺) into **C2b** (released) and **C2a**.
6. **C4b2a** forms on the surface — this is the **classical pathway C3 convertase**.
7. **C3 convertase cleaves C3** into:
   - **C3a** — anaphylatoxin
   - **C3b** — binds the membrane, the key **opsonin**
8. One C3b joins the convertase to give **C4b2a3b**, the **classical C5 convertase**.

**Membrane attack (lytic) unit — common terminal pathway**
9. **C5 is cleaved** into **C5a** (the most potent anaphylatoxin and chemotaxin) and **C5b**.
10. **C5b binds C6 and C7**, and the **C5b67** complex inserts into the lipid bilayer.
11. **C8** binds and begins membrane disruption.
12. **Multiple C9 molecules (10–16)** polymerise around it to form the **membrane attack complex (C5b-9)** — a **transmembrane pore of about 10 nm**.
13. The pore causes **loss of osmotic integrity, influx of water and ions, and cell lysis**.

**Regulation:** **C1 inhibitor (C1-INH)** blocks C1r and C1s (deficiency → **hereditary angio-oedema**); **C4-binding protein, factor I, DAF (CD55) and MCP (CD46)** dissociate or cleave the convertases; **CD59 (protectin)** blocks C9 polymerisation (deficiency with DAF → **paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria**).

## 805
**Answer**

**"The lectin and alternative pathways belong to innate immunity while the classical pathway belongs to acquired immunity" — explain**

**The classical pathway is a mechanism of acquired (adaptive) immunity because:**
1. It is **triggered by antigen–antibody complexes** — it **requires antibody (IgM or IgG)** to begin.
2. Antibody is the product of the **adaptive immune response**, produced by plasma cells only after antigen exposure, clonal selection and (usually) T cell help.
3. It therefore operates **only after a lag period** of days to weeks following first exposure, and acts with the **specificity and memory** of the adaptive system — on second exposure it is triggered rapidly and strongly.
4. It is an **effector arm of humoral immunity**: the antibody provides the specific recognition, and complement provides the destruction.

**The alternative and lectin pathways belong to innate immunity because:**
1. They are **antibody-independent** — they need no prior exposure and no adaptive response.
2. They are activated **directly by conserved microbial surface structures (PAMPs)**: **LPS, teichoic acid, peptidoglycan and zymosan** for the alternative pathway, and **mannose, GlcNAc and fucose residues** recognised by **mannose-binding lectin** for the lectin pathway.
3. They act **immediately on first contact**, within minutes to hours — providing **early defence before antibody is available**.
4. They are **non-specific** (the same response to any organism bearing the pattern) and show **no memory**.
5. **Self–non-self discrimination** is achieved structurally, not by specificity: mammalian cells **cap their surface sugars with sialic acid** and carry **regulators (DAF, MCP, factor H, CD59)**, so spontaneous C3b deposition is rapidly inactivated on host cells but amplified on microbial surfaces.
6. MBL is an **acute phase protein** and a classic germline-encoded **pattern recognition molecule**.

**Link between the two:** complement is one of the clearest bridges between the two arms — **C3d bound to antigen binds CR2 (CD21) on B cells, lowering the activation threshold about a thousandfold**, so innate complement activation directly **amplifies the adaptive antibody response**.

**Answer**

**Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC)** is a **cluster of closely linked, highly polymorphic genes whose products are cell-surface glycoproteins that present antigenic peptides to T lymphocytes**, and which determine the acceptance or rejection of tissue grafts between individuals.

- In humans it is called the **HLA (human leucocyte antigen) system**, located on the **short arm of chromosome 6 (6p21)**; in mice it is the **H-2 complex**.
- **Three regions:**
  - **Class I:** **HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C**
  - **Class II:** **HLA-DP, HLA-DQ, HLA-DR**
  - **Class III:** **complement components C2, C4, factor B**, TNF-α and β, heat shock proteins (not involved in antigen presentation)
- **Highly polymorphic** — the most polymorphic genes known — and **codominantly expressed**, inherited as a **haplotype** from each parent, which is why siblings have a **25% chance of being HLA-identical**.

## 810
**Answer**

**MHC antigens** are the **cell-surface glycoproteins encoded by the major histocompatibility complex**, which **bind and display processed antigenic peptides to T lymphocytes** and act as the **principal transplantation (histocompatibility) antigens** recognised as foreign in grafting.

- In humans these are the **HLA antigens**, encoded on **chromosome 6p21**.
- **MHC class I antigens (HLA-A, B, C):** a **heavy α chain of three domains non-covalently associated with β₂-microglobulin** (encoded on chromosome 15); the peptide groove is formed by α1 and α2 and holds **8–10 amino acid** peptides; present **endogenous** antigen to **CD8⁺** cells.
- **MHC class II antigens (HLA-DP, DQ, DR):** **two chains, α and β**, each of two domains; the groove is formed by **α1 and β1** and is open-ended, holding **13–18 amino acid** peptides; present **exogenous** antigen to **CD4⁺** cells.
- Both are **polygenic, highly polymorphic and codominantly expressed**.

5. **ABO grouping and a crossmatch** are always performed alongside.

## 812
**Answer**

**Biological importance of MHC antigens**
1. **Antigen presentation** — MHC molecules **bind processed peptides and display them to T cells**; T cells **cannot recognise free antigen**, so MHC is indispensable to all adaptive cell-mediated immunity.
2. **MHC restriction** — a T cell recognises antigen only when presented by **self MHC** (CD8 with class I, CD4 with class II), ensuring that T cells act only on the body's own cells.
3. **Thymic selection and self-tolerance** — in the thymus, **positive selection** retains thymocytes that bind self MHC with low affinity, and **negative selection** deletes those binding self peptide–MHC strongly, establishing **central tolerance**.
4. **Determination of immune responsiveness** — the MHC haplotype governs which peptides can be presented, and hence the strength of response to a given antigen (the Ir genes).
5. **Transplantation** — MHC molecules are the **major transplantation antigens**; mismatch provokes rejection, and the degree of matching predicts graft survival.
6. **Disease association** — **HLA-B27** (ankylosing spondylitis, 90×), **HLA-DR3/DR4** (type 1 diabetes), **HLA-DR2** (narcolepsy, Goodpasture's), **HLA-DQ2/DQ8** (coeliac disease), **HLA-B57:01** (abacavir hypersensitivity).
7. **NK cell regulation** — class I engages inhibitory receptors; its loss triggers killing ("missing self").
8. **Tumour immunity** — down-regulation of class I is a major **immune escape** mechanism of tumours and viruses.
9. **Paternity testing and anthropological studies**, owing to extreme polymorphism.
10. **Class III genes** encode **complement components C2, C4 and factor B, and TNF**.

**Role of MHC in antigen presentation and T/B cell activation**
- **Exogenous antigen** → endosomal processing → **MHC class II** → recognised by **CD4⁺ helper T cell** (signal 1), with **CD28–B7 co-stimulation** (signal 2) → T cell activation.
- **Endogenous (viral/tumour) antigen** → proteasome → TAP → ER → **MHC class I** → recognised by **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cell** → killing of the infected cell.
- **B cell activation:** the B cell binds native antigen by its surface immunoglobulin, **internalises and processes it, and presents the peptide on its own MHC class II** to a **Tfh cell**. The T cell then delivers **CD40L–CD40 signalling and cytokines (IL-4, IL-21)**, which drive **proliferation, class switching, affinity maturation and memory B cell formation** — this is the **linked (cognate) recognition** that underlies T-dependent antibody responses.

**Importance of the distribution:** because **every nucleated cell** bears class I, any virus-infected or tumour cell can be detected and destroyed by cytotoxic T cells; because class II is **confined to APCs**, helper T cell activation is restricted to the specialised cells equipped to deliver co-stimulation, preventing indiscriminate activation.

**Why MHC typing and matching are not required for corneal grafts and isografts**

**A. Isograft (syngeneic graft)** — between **genetically identical individuals**, i.e. **monozygotic (identical) twins** or inbred animals of the same strain.
- Donor and recipient have **exactly the same MHC/HLA genes**, so the graft tissue carries **no foreign histocompatibility antigen**.
- The recipient's T cells recognise it as **self**, there is no alloreactive response, and the graft is accepted **permanently without immunosuppression** — so typing is unnecessary.

**B. Corneal graft (keratoplasty)** — successful across HLA barriers because the cornea is an **immunologically privileged site**:
1. **Avascular** — the normal cornea has **no blood vessels**, so donor antigens are not carried to the regional lymph nodes and host lymphocytes and antibodies have no ready access (the **afferent and efferent arms of the response are both interrupted**).
2. **No lymphatic drainage**, so there is no route for antigen to reach lymphoid tissue.
3. **Low expression of MHC antigens** and **few resident antigen-presenting (Langerhans) cells** in the central cornea.
4. **Anterior chamber-associated immune deviation (ACAID)** — antigens entering the eye induce a **deviant, tolerogenic response** with suppression of delayed hypersensitivity.
5. The ocular environment is rich in **immunosuppressive factors** (TGF-β, α-MSH) and expresses **FasL**, inducing apoptosis of infiltrating lymphocytes.
- **Consequently, routine HLA matching is not required** and over 90% of first grafts succeed. Matching does become relevant in **vascularised (high-risk) corneas**, after inflammation or repeated grafting, where rejection rates rise sharply.
- *(Other privileged sites: brain, testis, pregnant uterus, anterior chamber of the eye.)*

## 815
**Answer**

**Role of MHC in antigen presentation**

T lymphocytes cannot recognise free, native antigen — they recognise only **processed peptide held in the groove of an MHC molecule on another cell**. This dual requirement is **MHC restriction**.

**A. The MHC class I (endogenous/cytosolic) pathway → CD8⁺ T cells**
1. **Source:** proteins synthesised **within** the cell — viral, tumour or self proteins
2. **Processing:** tagged with ubiquitin and degraded by the **proteasome** into **8–10 amino acid** peptides
3. **Transport:** carried into the **endoplasmic reticulum by TAP-1 and TAP-2**
4. **Loading:** the peptide binds the groove formed by the **α1 and α2 domains**, with calnexin, tapasin and β₂-microglobulin assembling the complex
5. **Display:** the peptide–MHC I complex moves via the Golgi to the surface of **any nucleated cell**
6. **Recognition:** the **TCR of a CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cell** engages it, with **CD8 binding the invariant α3 domain** → the cell is **killed** by perforin/granzyme
7. **Cross-presentation:** dendritic cells can also route exogenous antigen into this pathway to prime CD8 responses

**B. The MHC class II (exogenous/endosomal) pathway → CD4⁺ T cells**
1. **Source:** extracellular antigen taken up by **phagocytosis or endocytosis** by a professional APC
2. **Processing:** degraded by **acid proteases (cathepsins)** in the endosome into **13–18 amino acid** peptides
3. **Protection of the groove:** MHC II is synthesised in the ER with the **invariant chain (Ii)** blocking the groove, preventing premature binding of endogenous peptides
4. **Loading:** in the **MIIC compartment**, Ii is degraded to **CLIP**, which **HLA-DM** exchanges for the antigenic peptide
5. **Display:** the complex reaches the surface of the **dendritic cell, macrophage or B cell**
6. **Recognition:** the **TCR of a CD4⁺ helper T cell** engages it, with **CD4 binding the invariant β2 domain** → T cell activation, with **CD28–B7 co-stimulation** and cytokine signalling

**C. Structural basis:** the MHC groove is formed by **α1+α2** (class I, closed ends, short peptides) or **α1+β1** (class II, open ends, longer peptides). Polymorphic residues line the groove, so **each MHC allele binds a different set of peptides** — the molecular basis of **individual variation in immune responsiveness and of HLA–disease associations**.

**D. Consequence:** MHC class I surveys the **intracellular** compartment (viruses, tumours) and triggers **killing**; MHC class II surveys the **extracellular** compartment (bacteria, toxins) and triggers **help**.
