## 716
**Answer**

**Special features of mycobacteria**
1. **Acid-fastness** — once stained with hot carbol fuchsin they resist decolourisation by 20% sulphuric acid and alcohol, owing to the **high mycolic acid content** of the wall and its integrity.
2. **Lipid-rich cell wall (about 60% of dry weight)** — mycolic acids, cord factor (trehalose-6,6'-dimycolate), wax D, sulpholipids, mycosides; this gives **resistance to drying, acids, alkalis and many disinfectants**.
3. **Slow growth** — generation time of **15–20 hours** for *M. tuberculosis*, so cultures take **2–6 weeks** (*M. leprae* has not been grown on artificial media at all).
4. **Obligate aerobes**, non-motile, non-sporing, non-capsulated, **Gram-positive but poorly staining** ("ghosts").
5. **Intracellular survival in macrophages**, inhibiting phagosome–lysosome fusion.
6. **Highly antigenic cell wall components** — the basis of **delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity** and the tuberculin test.
7. **Resistance to antibiotics**, needing special **anti-tubercular drugs** in long, multi-drug regimens.
8. **Susceptible to heat (pasteurisation), UV light and 5% phenol**, but resistant to 10% H₂SO₄ and to drying in sputum.

## 720
**Answer**

**Laboratory diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis**

**1. Specimen:** **two sputum samples** (spot and early-morning), 5–10 mL, collected in a wide-mouth sterile container with full precautions. Alternatives: **gastric lavage** (children), bronchoalveolar lavage, laryngeal swab.

**2. Microscopy (the mainstay in programme settings)**
- **Ziehl–Neelsen stain:** **red, slender, beaded, slightly curved acid-fast bacilli** against a blue background; reported by the **grading of bacilli per field**. Needs **about 10⁴ bacilli/mL** to be positive.
- **Fluorescent (auramine–rhodamine) stain:** golden-yellow fluorescent bacilli; more sensitive and faster to read.
- **Concentration** by Petroff's method (4% NaOH) improves the yield.

**3. Culture — the gold standard**
- **Solid media:** **Löwenstein–Jensen (LJ)** medium — dry, rough, tough, buff-coloured colonies in **3–6 weeks**; Middlebrook 7H10/7H11.
- **Liquid/automated:** **MGIT 960, BACTEC** — growth in **10–14 days**, far faster.
- Culture detects as few as **10–100 bacilli/mL**, and allows **drug susceptibility testing**.

**4. Identification:** **niacin positive, nitrate reduction positive, heat-labile catalase (negative at 68 °C)**, no growth at 25 °C, susceptible to TCH (differentiating from *M. bovis*).

**5. Molecular (rapid)**
- **CBNAAT / GeneXpert MTB/RIF** — detects *M. tuberculosis* **and rifampicin resistance within 2 hours**; now the recommended initial test, especially in HIV and in suspected MDR.
- **Line probe assay (LPA)** for isoniazid and rifampicin resistance; PCR.

**6. Immunological**
- **Tuberculin (Mantoux) test** — indicates infection, not active disease.
- **IGRA (QuantiFERON, T-SPOT.TB)** — unaffected by BCG.
- Serology is **not recommended** by the WHO.

**7. Others:** chest X-ray (cavitation in upper lobes), histopathology of biopsy showing caseating granuloma, ESR, and HIV testing.

## 721
**Answer**

**Pathogenesis of primary pulmonary tuberculosis**
1. **Inhalation of droplet nuclei** containing 1–3 bacilli from a sputum-positive case; the particles are small enough to reach the **alveoli**, typically in the **lower part of the upper lobe or upper part of the lower lobe** (the best-ventilated zones).
2. **Phagocytosis by alveolar macrophages**, in which the bacilli **survive and multiply**, because **sulpholipids and the ESX-1 system inhibit phagosome–lysosome fusion**.
3. Unchecked intracellular multiplication over **2–4 weeks** forms the **subpleural "Ghon focus"** — a small area of exudative consolidation 1–2 cm across.
4. Bacilli drain along the **lymphatics to the hilar lymph nodes**, which enlarge. **Ghon focus + lymphangitis + hilar lymphadenopathy = the primary (Ghon) complex**.
5. During this stage there is a **silent, transient bacillaemia** seeding apices of the lungs, bone, kidneys, brain and other organs — the **Simon foci**, the basis of later reactivation.
6. At **4–6 weeks**, **cell-mediated immunity (CMI) and delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity** develop: **antigen-presenting cells → CD4⁺ Th1 cells → IFN-γ → macrophage activation**. The tuberculin test converts to positive.
7. Activated macrophages become **epithelioid cells and Langhans giant cells**, forming the **tubercle (granuloma)** surrounded by lymphocytes and fibroblasts.
8. The centre undergoes **caseous necrosis**, in which the low oxygen tension and acidity restrict bacillary growth but allow **dormant survival**.
9. In **over 90% of immunocompetent people** the lesion is contained, heals by **fibrosis and calcification**, and leaves only a positive tuberculin test and a calcified nodule.
10. In the remainder it progresses — **progressive primary tuberculosis**, cavitation, **miliary spread**, tuberculous meningitis or pleural effusion, particularly in infants, the malnourished and the immunosuppressed.

## 722
**Answer**

**Pathogenesis of tubercular meningitis (TBM)**
1. TBM is almost always **secondary to tuberculosis elsewhere**, most often a primary pulmonary focus in children or reactivation in adults.
2. During the **bacillaemia of primary infection**, bacilli seed the **meninges, subpial and subependymal cortex**, forming small subcortical tubercles — the **"Rich foci"**.
3. After a latent period, a **Rich focus ruptures into the subarachnoid space**, discharging tubercular antigen and bacilli into the CSF.
4. This provokes an intense **delayed hypersensitivity response**, producing a **thick, gelatinous exudate** that is most marked at the **base of the brain** (basal meningitis) around the interpeduncular fossa and optic chiasma.
5. Three consequences follow:
   - **Cranial nerve entrapment** in the basal exudate — especially **III, IV, VI and VII** → ophthalmoplegia, squint, facial palsy
   - **Obliterative endarteritis** of the perforating vessels → **infarction** of the basal ganglia and internal capsule → hemiplegia, movement disorders
   - **Blockage of CSF pathways** (basal cisterns, aqueduct) → **communicating hydrocephalus** and raised intracranial pressure
6. **Clinically** the onset is **insidious over 2–3 weeks**: prodrome of fever, malaise, irritability and personality change (stage I), then headache, vomiting, neck stiffness and cranial nerve palsies (stage II), then coma, hemiplegia and decerebrate posturing (stage III).
7. **Outcome** is determined by the stage at which treatment starts — hence the emphasis on early suspicion.

**Laboratory diagnosis**
1. **CSF by lumbar puncture:** **clear or slightly opalescent, forms a "cobweb" (spider web) coagulum** on standing; pressure raised.
2. **Cytology:** **100–500 cells/µL, predominantly lymphocytes** (polymorphs may dominate early).
3. **Biochemistry:** **protein markedly raised (100–500 mg/dL)**, **glucose moderately reduced**, **chloride markedly reduced**.
4. **Microscopy:** **Ziehl–Neelsen stain of the cobweb coagulum or centrifuged deposit** — yield is low (10–20%), improved by examining a large volume for 30 minutes.
5. **Culture:** LJ medium (6 weeks) or **MGIT liquid culture** (2 weeks) — definitive but slow.
6. **Molecular: CBNAAT/GeneXpert MTB/RIF on CSF** — the rapid test of choice, also giving rifampicin resistance; PCR.
7. **Adenosine deaminase (ADA)** in CSF — raised, supportive.
8. **Imaging:** CT/MRI shows **basal exudate, hydrocephalus, infarcts and tuberculomas**.
9. **Supporting:** chest X-ray for a primary focus, tuberculin test, HIV testing.
*(Treatment must be started on clinical suspicion — delay is the main determinant of death and disability.)*

## 729
**Answer**

**The tuberculin (Mantoux) test**

- **Principle:** a **delayed (type IV) hypersensitivity** skin test that detects **prior sensitisation to mycobacterial protein** — that is, **infection**, not necessarily active disease.
- **Material:** **PPD (purified protein derivative)**, **5 tuberculin units (0.1 mL)**, with Tween 80 to prevent adsorption.
- **Method:** injected **intradermally** on the flexor surface of the forearm, raising a **pale wheal of 6–10 mm**; read at **48–72 hours**.
- **Reading:** measure the **transverse diameter of INDURATION (not erythema)** in millimetres, by palpation or the ballpoint-pen method.

**Interpretation**

| Induration | Significance |
|---|---|
| **< 5 mm** | **Negative** — no sensitisation (or a false negative) |
| **5–9 mm** | **Doubtful/positive in high-risk groups** — HIV, recent contact, immunosuppressed, organ transplant, fibrotic changes on X-ray |
| **≥ 10 mm** | **Positive** — recent immigrants, IV drug users, health workers, children under 4, diabetes, chronic renal failure, silicosis |
| **≥ 15 mm** | **Positive in anyone**, including those with no risk factor |

- A **positive test** means infection with *M. tuberculosis* **at some time** (or BCG vaccination, or atypical mycobacterial exposure) — in an endemic country it does **not** by itself diagnose active disease. In a **child under 5 with symptoms** it is strong supporting evidence.
- **Conversion** — an increase of **≥10 mm within 2 years** — indicates **recent infection**.
- A **strongly positive reaction with vesiculation and necrosis** suggests active disease.
- **False positives:** BCG vaccination, infection with atypical mycobacteria.
- **False negatives:** miliary TB and TBM, HIV, malnutrition, measles and other viral infections, steroids and immunosuppression, lymphoma, sarcoidosis, the 4–6 week window period, and technical errors (see Q730).
- **Booster phenomenon:** a waned reaction revived by repeat testing, which may be mistaken for conversion — avoided by **two-step testing**.

## 732
**Answer**

**Importance of BCG vaccination**

**BCG (Bacille Calmette–Guérin)** is a **live attenuated vaccine** derived from *Mycobacterium bovis*, attenuated by Calmette and Guérin over 13 years and 231 subcultures on glycerine–potato–bile medium.

1. **Protection against severe childhood tuberculosis** — its greatest value: it reliably prevents **miliary tuberculosis and tuberculous meningitis** in infants (about **80% protection**), the forms with the highest mortality.

6. **Other uses:** **intravesical BCG for superficial bladder carcinoma** (immunotherapy); some protection against **leprosy** and Buruli ulcer.
7. **Contraindications:** symptomatic HIV, immunodeficiency, immunosuppressive therapy, generalised eczema, pregnancy.
8. **Complications:** persistent ulcer, **BCG adenitis**, osteomyelitis, and **disseminated BCG-osis** in the immunodeficient.

## 741
**Answer**

**Special properties of *Treponema pallidum***
1. **Morphology:** slender spiral organism, **6–14 regular coils**, 0.1–0.2 µm thick and 6–15 µm long, with **pointed ends**; too thin to be seen by ordinary light microscopy or Gram stain.
2. **Motility:** characteristic **flexion, rotation around the long axis and backward-forward (corkscrew) movements**, produced by the **axial filament/endoflagella**; demonstrated by **dark-field microscopy**.
3. **Cannot be cultured on artificial media** — it is maintained by **intratesticular inoculation in rabbits (the Nichols strain)**.
4. **Extremely delicate:** killed rapidly by **drying, heat (41–42 °C), soap and water, antiseptics, and in stored blood at 4 °C for 72 hours** — hence transmission essentially requires **direct, intimate contact**.
5. **Microaerophilic**, with a slow **generation time of about 30–33 hours**, which accounts for the long incubation period and the need for prolonged (long-acting) penicillin therapy.
6. **Remains exquisitely sensitive to penicillin**, with **no resistance recorded** to date.
7. Has a **sparse outer membrane with few surface proteins** ("stealth pathogen"), which helps it evade the immune response and persist.
8. **Staining:** demonstrated by **silver impregnation (Fontana's method)**, immunofluorescence, or dark-field; it does **not** take up Gram stain.
9. Pathogenicity is **not toxin-mediated** — damage results from the **host's inflammatory and immune response** (endarteritis obliterans).

**Modes of transmission of syphilis**
1. **Sexual contact** — the principal route (acquired syphilis); infectivity is greatest in the primary and secondary stages
2. **Transplacental (vertical)** — after the fourth month of gestation → **congenital syphilis**
3. **Blood transfusion** — from fresh, infected blood ("syphilis d'emblée", no primary chancre)
4. **Direct contact** with an open lesion — kissing, and accidental inoculation in health workers (occupational)
5. Rarely through contaminated instruments or fomites

**Stages of syphilis with characteristic lesions**

**1. Primary syphilis** (incubation 9–90 days, average 3 weeks)
- **Hard chancre (Hunterian chancre)** at the site of inoculation — genitalia, cervix, lips, anus
- **Painless, indurated ("button-like"), clean-based, single ulcer with a rolled margin**, exuding clear serum teeming with treponemes
- **Painless, rubbery, non-suppurative regional lymphadenopathy** ("bubo")
- Heals spontaneously in 3–8 weeks without scarring, even untreated — **highly infectious**

**2. Secondary syphilis** (6–8 weeks after the chancre; the stage of **dissemination**)
- **Hallmarks:** a **symmetrical, non-itchy, coppery-red maculopapular rash involving the palms and soles** — unlike almost any other rash
- **Condylomata lata** — moist, broad, highly infectious wart-like lesions at warm moist sites
- **Mucous patches** and "snail-track ulcers" in the mouth and throat
- **Generalised non-tender lymphadenopathy**, moth-eaten alopecia, fever, malaise
- **The most infectious stage**; serology is positive in nearly 100%

**3. Latent syphilis**
- **No clinical signs, only positive serology**; divided into **early latent (<1 year, may relapse and is infectious)** and **late latent (>1 year, non-infectious except transplacentally)**

**4. Tertiary (late) syphilis** (3–20 years later, in about a third of untreated cases)
- **Hallmark: the gumma** — a **localised, granulomatous, necrotic lesion with central coagulative necrosis**, in skin, bone, liver, testis; few treponemes, driven by **delayed hypersensitivity**
- **Cardiovascular syphilis:** **aortitis of the ascending aorta** from endarteritis of the vasa vasorum → **aortic aneurysm and aortic incompetence**
- **Neurosyphilis:** meningovascular syphilis, **tabes dorsalis** (posterior column degeneration, lightning pains, Argyll Robertson pupil), **general paresis of the insane**
- **Non-infectious**

**5. Congenital syphilis**
- **Early:** snuffles, bullous rash (pemphigus syphiliticus), hepatosplenomegaly, osteochondritis
- **Late (stigmata):** **Hutchinson's triad** — notched incisors, interstitial keratitis and eighth nerve deafness; plus saddle nose, sabre tibia, mulberry molars, rhagades

**Hard chancre explained:** the primary lesion of syphilis — a **single, painless, indurated ulcer with a clean base and raised rolled edges**, so called because the base feels **cartilage-hard on palpation** due to **dense dermal lymphocytic and plasma cell infiltration with endarteritis obliterans**. Its serum is loaded with treponemes and is the ideal specimen for dark-field microscopy. (Contrast: the **soft sore/chancroid** of *Haemophilus ducreyi* is **painful, multiple, non-indurated with ragged undermined edges** and tender suppurative nodes.)

**Answer**

**Short note: VDRL test**

- **Full form:** **Venereal Disease Research Laboratory** test — a **non-treponemal (reaginic) slide flocculation test** for syphilis.
- **Principle:** the serum of a syphilitic patient contains a non-specific antibody, **"reagin" (IgG and IgM)**, directed against **lipids released from damaged host cells and from treponemes**. It reacts with the **VDRL antigen — cardiolipin, lecithin and cholesterol** — to give visible **flocculation**.
- **Method:** patient's serum is **inactivated at 56 °C for 30 minutes**, mixed with freshly prepared antigen on a glass slide, rotated at **180 rpm for 4 minutes**, and read **microscopically (10×)** for clumps. Quantitated by serial doubling dilutions; the titre is the highest dilution giving flocculation.
- **Results:** reactive, weakly reactive or non-reactive.
- **Advantages:** cheap, rapid, simple, suited to **mass screening**; **quantitative, so it monitors response to treatment** (a fourfold fall = cure); the **only test validated on CSF** for neurosyphilis.
- **Limitations**
  - **Not specific** — **biological false positives** in SLE, antiphospholipid syndrome, leprosy, malaria, pregnancy, viral infections, old age
  - **False negative in early primary syphilis** (before 1–4 weeks) and in **late/latent syphilis**
  - **Prozone phenomenon** in secondary syphilis with very high antibody titre — the test must be repeated on diluted serum
  - Needs a microscope and freshly prepared antigen daily
- **A reactive VDRL must always be confirmed by a treponemal test (TPHA or FTA-ABS).**

**Pathogenesis of *Mycoplasma* infection**
1. **Mycoplasmas are the smallest free-living organisms** and the **only bacteria that completely lack a cell wall**, being bounded by a **triple-layered membrane containing sterols**. They are therefore **pleomorphic**, **not visible on Gram stain**, and **intrinsically resistant to all cell-wall-active antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins, vancomycin)**.
2. ***M. pneumoniae*** is transmitted by **droplet infection**, spreading slowly within closed communities (families, schools, military recruits), with an incubation period of 2–3 weeks.
3. **Adherence** is the critical first step: the organism attaches to the **base of the cilia of respiratory epithelium** by the **P1 adhesin protein** at a specialised **terminal attachment organelle**, binding sialoglycoprotein receptors. This resists mucociliary clearance.
4. **Direct cytotoxicity:** it generates **hydrogen peroxide and superoxide radicals**, which damage the ciliated epithelium, causing **ciliostasis, loss of cilia and epithelial desquamation** — hence the persistent, hacking dry cough.
5. **CARDS toxin** (community-acquired respiratory distress syndrome toxin) ADP-ribosylates host proteins, causing vacuolation and ciliary damage.
6. The organism is essentially an **extracellular surface parasite**, though it can survive intracellularly, which contributes to persistence and relapse.
7. **Immunopathology is prominent:** the host inflammatory response, with lymphocyte and plasma cell infiltration of the peribronchial tissue, is largely responsible for the pneumonia — the **radiological changes are typically far worse than the clinical signs** ("walking pneumonia").
8. **Autoimmune phenomena** arise from cross-reaction between mycoplasmal glycolipid antigens and human antigens:
   - **Cold agglutinins (anti-I antibody)** → **autoimmune haemolytic anaemia** (the basis of the cold agglutinin test)
   - Cross-reactive antibodies → **erythema multiforme/Stevens–Johnson syndrome**, myocarditis, arthritis, **Guillain–Barré syndrome** and other neurological complications
9. **Other species:** ***M. hominis*** and ***Ureaplasma urealyticum*** colonise the genital tract, causing **pelvic inflammatory disease, postpartum fever and non-gonococcal urethritis**; ***M. genitalium*** causes persistent NGU.
10. **Treatment** must use drugs acting other than on the cell wall — **macrolides, tetracyclines or fluoroquinolones**.

**Probable diagnosis:** **Acute pyogenic bacterial meningitis caused by *Haemophilus influenzae* type b** — the CSF shows pus cells with **Gram-negative coccobacilli**, which in a young child is characteristic of Hib. *(Pleomorphic Gram-negative coccobacilli distinguish it from the Gram-negative diplococci of meningococcus.)*

**Medium for culture and the reason**

- **Chocolate agar**, incubated at **37 °C in 5–10% CO₂** for 24–48 hours.
- **Reason:** *H. influenzae* is a **fastidious organism** that requires two accessory growth factors present in blood:
  - **Factor X (haemin)** — heat-stable, needed for cytochrome synthesis
  - **Factor V (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, NAD)** — heat-labile, and in whole blood it is **bound to intracellular NADase inhibitors**
- In **ordinary blood agar the red cells are intact**, so factor V remains inside them and is unavailable, and the organism **fails to grow**. **Heating the blood to 70–80 °C ("chocolatising")** lyses the red cells, **releasing both factors X and V** and simultaneously **destroying the NADase** that would otherwise degrade factor V.
- **Levinthal's and Fildes' agar** are alternatives; **satellitism** around a *Staphylococcus aureus* streak on blood agar (which supplies factor V) is a useful confirmatory demonstration.
- Confirmation is by **growth requirement for X and V factors**, **capsular typing (type b) by latex agglutination or the Quellung reaction**, and **PCR**.
